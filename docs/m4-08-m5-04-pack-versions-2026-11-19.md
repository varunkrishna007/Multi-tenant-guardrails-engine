# `insurance@3`, `energy@3`, `finance@2`, `healthcare@2` (decided 2026-11-19)

Thresholds from the 2026-11-19 08:00Z live runs. **Every value below is provisional.** The
evidence is in-sample, synthetic and template-heavy, with no held-out check and no real traffic. Nothing here is a final threshold, and a pack version never changes once shipped: every
earlier version (`pack.json` = @1, `pack.v2.json` of insurance and energy = @2) is byte for byte
what shipped, and a test pins their hashes.

* **Derived, not hand-edited.** `uv run python -m packs.derive_versions` writes the four files
  from the base version and the run's records; `--check` (also a test) fails if they drift.
* **Only thresholds change.** `version`, `block_at`, `escalate_at` and `provisional` are the only
  fields that differ from the base version (@2 for insurance and energy, @1 for finance and
  healthcare). The questions, criteria, guard points, modes, severities and caps are the base's.
* **Notes, with the arithmetic and the confusion tables:**
  `calibration/results/m4-08-insurance-v2-remeasure-r3.md`, `m4-08-energy-v2-remeasure-r3.md`,
  `m5-04-finance-live-3.md`, `m5-04-healthcare-live-r2.md`, produced by
  `uv run python -m calibration.write_results` (a test checks each committed note is what the
  records give).
* **Spend:** 1,870 checks, 1,941 provider calls, about $0.21. No refusal, no auth failure, no 429,
  one timeout (re-attempted once, answered).

## The rules these use

* **`block_at`** is the midpoint between the highest hard-pool negative and the lowest positive
  (each the mean of the row's three draws; the hard pool is the rule's own category), rounded to
  0.01, clamped to [0.50, 0.90], and never below the B2 suggestion on the all-rows pool when that
  is higher than the midpoint.
* **The B2 all-rows floor leaves out another rule's positives** (decided 2026-11-19, every pack): a
  row that is a positive of another **enforce-mode, blocking** rule at the same guard point is not
  a false positive, because blocking it is right. Benign rows count, and so do the positives of
  escalate-only rules. Precision and the gates are computed on the same basis. Without it the
  floor sat at 0.81 to 0.95 on four finance rules and at 0.93 and 0.94 on two healthcare rules,
  only because rows of neighbouring rules scored high, and it cost per-draw recall (for example
  `d__fin_personal_advice` at 0.95: 46 of 48 draws).
* **`escalate_at`** is `block_at` - 0.30, at least 0.30, at least 0.05 above the highest hard-pool
  negative, and at least 0.10 below `block_at`.
* **Escalate-only rules** (`d__fin_scam_signal`, `d__hc_emergency_symptoms`, `d__en_safety_report`,
  `d__ins_fraud_signal`) keep their `escalate_at`; their `block_at` stays the base's and is inert
  (the cap holds every action at escalate).
* **A rule with no sendable positive keeps the base's values.** `d__hc_dosage_advice` is
  **not calibrated live**: every row that fires it is withheld from the provider, so
  nothing was measured. `d__ins_fraud_signal` and `d__en_unsafe_advice` are the same.

## What the data show, and what it does not

* At these values, on the rows sent, every calibrated rule has block precision 1.00, escalate
  recall 1.00 and no false action, on both pools, row means and single draws alike.
* **Most rules' score gap is wide**: on the all pool most positives score above 0.89 and most
  benign rows below 0.15, so the clamp floor and the 0.30 floor decide most values, not the data.
  A midpoint of 0.50 and an escalate_at of 0.30 are the agreed limits, not measured optima. The
  exceptions are the rules to watch: `d__hc_record_change` (highest benign row 0.538),
  `d__en_price_certainty` (highest benign 0.452, lowest positive 0.804), `d__fin_tax_legal_conclusion`
  (lowest positive 0.675, so its 0.50 block_at has only 0.175 of room below the lowest positive) and
  `d__hc_phi_disclosure` (lowest positive 0.751).
* The scores come from the nonce-averaged draws of one model on synthetic, template-heavy rows
  written by one author. A rule with a threshold near the benign maximum (`d__hc_record_change`)
  is the one to watch on real traffic.
* For the two re-measures, the all pool is the rows sent (the rules' own categories and the rows
  a named rule scored 0.5 or more on last time), not the whole labelled set. Most of those
  cross-fire rows are then left out by the exclusion above, and the notes list them per rule.
* **The exclusion hides no cross-rule firing, and the 54 rows are owed a read.** Each note gives,
  per rule, the excluded rows' count, highest score and how many sit at or above the new
  `block_at`, with their ids. On finance and healthcare, 54 rows of other rules score at or above
  a rule's new `block_at`: `d__fin_personal_advice` 23 of 48 (highest 0.945), `d__hc_diagnosis`
  10 of 30 (0.925), `d__fin_pressure_sale` 9 of 48 (0.835), `d__fin_guaranteed_return` 6 of 48
  (0.918), `d__fin_tax_legal_conclusion` 4 of 48 (0.806), `d__hc_outcome_promise` 2 of 30
  (0.935). Insurance, energy, `d__hc_phi_disclosure` and `d__hc_dosage_advice` have none. Decided
  (2026-11-19): they do **not** block shipping @2, because the thresholds are about the action and
  blocking those rows is right, but they are blocked with a second rule's reason too. **A
  row-by-row read** (dual-label the row, or tighten the rule's description, which needs a later
  live re-measure) **is owed before any rule's `provisional` flag drops**, together with the
  two-person review of the labels the golden sets are owed.
* **Not done:** no held-out set, no real traffic, no change to `pack_live` (it still sends @1 and
  @2 only); the @2 files of insurance and energy stay in the catalog, and a deployment lists the
  files it wants in `catalog.pack_files` (a published version is never removed).
